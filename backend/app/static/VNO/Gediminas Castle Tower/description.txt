Welcome to one of the most iconic symbols of Vilnius – the Gediminas Castle Tower. Standing proudly on Gediminas Hill, this red-brick tower is all that remains of the Upper Castle, built in the early 15th century by Grand Duke Vytautas. But its story goes even further back: the hill was first fortified in the 14th century by Grand Duke Gediminas himself, the legendary founder of Vilnius.

From here, you get one of the best panoramic views of the city – the Old Town’s baroque churches on one side, and the modern skyscrapers of the business district on the other. It’s almost like seeing the past and present of Lithuania in one glance.

Inside the tower, you’ll find a small but fascinating museum, showcasing medieval armor, archaeological finds, and models of Vilnius through the centuries. And don’t miss the Lithuanian national flag flying at the top – it has been proudly raised here since 1988, during the independence movement.

So, as you stand here, imagine the dukes, soldiers, and townsfolk who once looked out from this same hill, guarding their city. Today, you get to enjoy the same view, only with fewer arrows flying around!
